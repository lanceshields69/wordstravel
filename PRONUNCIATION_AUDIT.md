# Pronunciation audit — Words Travel

Audited 2026-10-09 against `content/word-journeys.json`, `src/JourneyCard.tsx`, and `src/pronunciation.ts`. Scope: **12 words, 72 journey cards**. This is a content and code audit, not an acoustic verification. The user reported that Sanskrit and Medieval Latin said “Reading awaits review,” Arabic on Zero and Sugar was silent, Persian was unavailable, and Italian, French, and English worked on their device. Six user-recorded Typhoon MP3s were added and linked after the initial audit; they are inventoried below and available in the [local listening preview](reference/typhoon-audio-preview.html). User-recorded Magazine Arabic, Coffee Arabic/Turkish, and Sugar Sanskrit/Persian/Arabic/Medieval Latin MP3s were linked later. The content owner reports that Typhoon Ancient Greek and Sugar Sanskrit/Medieval Latin readings were reviewed, but the sources or specialists are not recorded here, and this audit did not independently listen to or verify any file. Ketchup Hokkien and Orange Sanskrit/Persian/Arabic/Italian MP3s are present but unlinked. Historical reading review is still needed for Hokkien and Orange Sanskrit. Labels such as “Generated modern pronunciation” in the JSON describe editorial intent; they do **not** mean that a reading was verified.

## How the current app decides whether to play

- **Editorial hold (14 cards) and three approved recordings:** 17 cards have `generation.language: null`. Fourteen remain blocked and say “Reading awaits review,” even if a file URL is added without approval. Typhoon Ancient Greek and Sugar Sanskrit/Medieval Latin have explicit `owner_approved_audio: true` and play only their supplied MP3s; they have no browser speech fallback. All 72 cards also have `review_status: research_draft`; that broad field does not control the button.
- **Browser voice conditional (55 cards):** a non-null `generation.language` permits a modern illustrative reading only when the device exposes a matching `speechSynthesis` voice. Region-specific tags such as `fa-IR` and `en-GB` now require that exact locale; a base tag such as `ar` can use `ar` or an `ar-*` voice. No unrelated or different-region voice is selected silently. The chosen browser locale is exposed in the button label and title. `yue` does not automatically use `zh-HK` because that mapping needs content review.
- **Playback is separate from voice discovery:** a browser can list a voice that fails to start, errors, or produces no audible sound. The code now waits for delayed voice loading, checks the voice again at click time, reports error codes and an eight-second no-start timeout, and prevents a canceled previous card from reporting an error on the new card. An audible result and accurate pronunciation still need listening tests. The six linked Typhoon MP3s take precedence over browser speech and report file playback failure instead of silently falling back.
- **Research categories:** 14 active content holds, three owner-approved historical recordings, 16 voice-eligible cards whose historical form or bilingual display needs research, and 39 modern illustrative candidates. The 16 remain technically playable as **modern approximations**, not historical reconstructions. Their current eligibility is not editorial verification.

## Confirmed defects and fixes

1. **Delayed voice discovery:** the old UI stopped checking after 900 ms. It listened for `voiceschanged`, but browsers can populate the list late or without a useful event. The UI now subscribes before its first read, polls for three seconds, and continues listening for later changes. A missing voice produces a specific message. [MDN documents the `voiceschanged` event](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesis/voiceschanged_event).
2. **Listed voice with no sound:** the old adapter set “playing” before the utterance started and had no timeout. The adapter now handles `start`, `end`, and `error`; if speech has not started in eight seconds, it cancels and shows a clear error. It distinguishes `voice-unavailable` and `not-allowed` from a generic failure. [MDN lists speech error codes](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesisErrorEvent/error). This detects missing events, not inaudible output after a successful start event.
3. **Locale fallback and stale voice:** the old matcher could silently use `en-US` for `en-GB`, or another same-language region. Matching is now exact for a requested regional locale and limited to same-language locales only for bare tags such as `ar`. The selected voice is checked again when clicked. Voice selection alone cannot prove that an engine used the requested voice audibly.
4. **Audio field ignored:** the content schema included `audio_url`, but playback never read it. Eligible cards now play a populated file first, surface file errors, and never silently fall back to a browser voice. An unapproved `generation.language: null` card remains held even if a file URL is added; the Typhoon Ancient Greek recording has a specific owner-approved exception.
5. **Card transition:** the pronunciation control is keyed by card ID, so a previous card’s message does not remain visible on the next card. Cancellation detaches old callbacks before stopping playback.

## Batch recording proposal — no audio generated in this audit

For predictable playback, prepare a manifest of the **39 modern illustrative candidates** (card ID, approved text, locale, voice, output path, reviewer, and version). Prioritize the **12** candidates using `ar` (7), `fa-IR` (1), `nl-NL` (1), `es-MX` (1), `ms-MY` (1), and `pt-PT` (1), since the user observed Arabic silence/Persian absence and the other locales may be scarce in browser voice lists. The 18 English and 9 Italian/French cards can continue using browser speech on the reported device; recording them is optional if consistent cross-device sound is desired. These counts are candidates for modern illustration, not verified pronunciations.

One practical route is [Azure Speech Batch Synthesis](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/batch-synthesis): its current API accepts short text asynchronously, and [batch properties](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/batch-synthesis-properties) allow multiple SSML inputs, one output file per input, and selectable output formats. Before submitting a paid job, query the [resource’s voice-list endpoint](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/rest-text-to-speech#get-a-list-of-voices) for the chosen region, choose a locale-specific voice per card, and confirm current price/rights. The published [language and voice catalog](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support) includes Arabic, Persian, Malay, and Cantonese options, but catalog support does **not** validate a particular word’s reading. Generate to a temporary directory, map outputs back by manifest/card ID, have a qualified human listen for pronunciation and dialect, then place approved clips under `public/audio/` and set `audio_url`. Keep provider credentials out of the browser bundle. No job, API call, or paid audio generation was made here.

**Historical work is separate.** The 14 remaining holds include other Sanskrit and Medieval Latin stages, Ancient/Byzantine Greek, Latin, Old English, Hokkien, and Iranian/Avestan comparison stages. Define the intended period, region, script, and scholarly reading convention; gather pronunciation scholarship and specialist review; then commission or record a reviewed reading. Typhoon Ancient Greek and Sugar Sanskrit/Medieval Latin recordings are enabled on the content owner's reported review; documenting their sources or specialists is still needed. The 16 additionally flagged cards include Old French spellings, historical Persian/Arabic/Catalan/Turkish forms, a disputed Cantonese comparison, and the mixed Hindi/Urdu display. A modern TTS voice may be useful as an explicitly labeled illustration but does not reconstruct those stages. `typhoon-indic` still needs a separate Urdu reading; `check-arabic` needs review of Arabic transmission of the Persian-derived form; `typhoon-chinese` still needs a decision about the Cantonese comparison and voice tag. Do not clear another `language: null` hold or label any output “verified” merely because a provider supports the language. Reviewed historical audio is represented by an explicit owner-approved file state rather than inferred from `review_status: research_draft` or a non-null language tag.
